# Damp asphalt and authored tyre wear

All 18 Formula career circuits use a restrained wet sheen on their asphalt and a pair of subtle grooved tyre traces following the authored driving route. Corner deposits are stronger than the wear on straights. These are pre-existing circuit wear marks, rather than tracks generated by each car during play.

## Materials

25 authored asphalt material variants keep the standard URP Lit shader, original albedo/road markings and existing normal/detail maps. A seamless linear smoothness mask replaces the former near-zero smoothness mask; RGB is zero to keep the road dielectric. Damp circuits use a 0.76 smoothness multiplier, and the existing rain circuit uses 0.90. The mask reduces the resulting smoothness and varies it across the surface. Specular lighting and existing environment reflections provide the sheen. No pipeline settings, reflection cameras, probes, SSR or physics properties were changed.

The existing `Asphalt Smoothness.png` GUID, source dimensions and Switch ASTC 6x6 importer override are preserved. Road variants now sample that shared mask once in their existing Lit pass; aggregate variants already used a mask. The original vendor textures are unchanged. `FormulaTrackSurfaceBuilder` uses the same finish settings when surface materials are authored again.

## Static tyre traces

`FormulaAsphaltFinishAuthoring` projects two 32 cm tyre strips along the circuit waypoints, using the road colliders to select the correct elevation and surface normal. Route interpolation, small lateral variation, varying opacity, soft edges and longitudinal grooves soften the pattern. A closed-loop UV interval prevents a tread seam at the lap boundary. Vertices sit 12 mm above the sampled surface, with depth bias for grazing views.

Each scene contains one `Formula Authored Tyre Wear` root with only Transform, MeshFilter and MeshRenderer. It references one non-readable mesh with one submesh and one shared material. The custom URP shader has one unlit multiplicative pass and one texture sample; it attenuates the existing lit road and fades neutrally through fog. Shadows, light probes, reflection probes and motion-vector generation are disabled on the wear renderer. No runtime generation, raycasts, extra colliders or update scripts are added.

## Cost

- One additional forward draw per visible circuit wear renderer in a single-camera view; additional views can render it again.
- 2,972–14,620 vertices per loaded circuit, at most 438,576 bytes of raw vertex/index data. Mesh CPU readability is disabled.
- A shared 64×256 tread texture, approximately 10.3 KiB at Switch ASTC 6x6 including mips.
- The shared 1024² smoothness mask is approximately 0.60 MiB at Switch ASTC 6x6 including mips. It was already resident on circuits using aggregate materials and becomes an additional dependency where it was not previously used.
- No new render targets, realtime reflection captures or per-car wear objects.

These are structural/data estimates, excluding engine overhead and target GPU timing. They are not a Switch memory or FPS measurement. Only the current circuit's wear mesh is loaded with its scene.

## Validation

- Unity 6000.3.19f1 compiled the Editor tooling and rendered the shaders on Metal without new compilation/runtime errors.
- All 18 scenes were authored and reopened: exactly one correctly bound wear renderer per circuit, active authored asphalt, valid shared materials, one-pass wear shader and non-readable wear meshes.
- Every sampled strip edge projected onto the road: zero missed samples on all 18 circuits. No whole-circuit visual flythrough is claimed.
- Before/after images cover five representative circuits, two views each: ordinary asphalt, leaf-covered asphalt, rain, the converted desert track and RTG asphalt. Reviewed images are under `Before/` and `After/`.
- Real menu → Time Attack → menu smoke tests passed on `track_01` and `track_06`, with AI-controlled solo physics advancing 316 metres over 12 simulated seconds on each, upright driving, supported/visible wear rendering and no errors. These are loading/rendering checks, not full-lap handling tests. An earlier mixed-grid rendering smoke also loaded/rendered successfully but its automated driver collided with other cars and overturned in the wet run; that evidence is retained in `MixedGridSmoke/` and is not treated as a handling pass.
- `SceneIntegrity.json` confirms all pre-existing serialized scene objects are byte-identical to the task-start versions. Only four native object documents and one scene-root reference were added per circuit. Previous tree/arrow corrections and the user's build-settings/font changes are preserved.

No Nintendo SDK/module is installed on this Mac. A new Windows Switch build is required for target shader compilation and hardware performance evaluation.

## Reauthoring

Use `Tools/Formula Career/Surfaces/Apply Damp Asphalt And Tyre Wear` in Edit Mode after changing road geometry or rebuilding circuits. Save scene edits first. The tool writes its results progressively and reuses existing mesh/material GUIDs rather than adding duplicate wear roots. Read-only asset/binding checks are under `Validate Damp Asphalt Assets`; the isolated-save sun/rain smoke test is under `Play Validate Damp Asphalt`.

The one-time execution trigger used during implementation was removed. Opening the project does not author scenes or start tests automatically.
