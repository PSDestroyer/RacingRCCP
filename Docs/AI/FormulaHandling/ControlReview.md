# Formula control review — 2026-10-26

Goal: a controllable stock Generic Formula car with progression adding performance and composure. All nine Generic prefabs and their builder share the correction.

Follow-up: sustained full-throttle cornering exposed a separate power-oversteer problem after these steering/chassis changes. `FormulaTractionControl` now regulates temporary engine torque while TCS is enabled. See [PowerCornerReview.md](PowerCornerReview.md) and `TractionValidation*.json` for the diagnosis and production acceptance. The 86-case cornering matrix below used 25% throttle and is historical steering/chassis evidence, not validation of the full-throttle correction.

## Findings and changes

- The earlier speed-sensitive steering curve restricted sustained turning too strongly. RCCP's fixed rack speed also reached the remaining high-speed steering lock in one physics tick. The new curve restores turning range; `FormulaSteering` scales rack travel to the available lock (0.24 s outward, 0.12 s returning, 0.16 s reversing). It uses the existing processed input for every device and leaves AI/external control ownership intact.
- Zero-steering acceleration exposed a separate suspension/load instability after several seconds. Disabling TCS or changing the differential did not solve it. The selected calibration changes wheel damping from 8500 to 4500 and force application distance from 0.05 to 0.2. Spring rates, grip, torque and speed limit are unchanged.
- Two direct `COM` children existed. RCCP resolved the first, at `(0, 0.3150252, 0)`, rather than the intended `(0, 0.25, -0.15)`. Authoring now retains one correctly positioned COM; the builder reuses it.
- The existing front-wheel centering correction is preserved. Diagnostics measured positive body clearance, so chassis scraping is not supported as the cause. The exact native physics mechanism behind the damper-dependent instability has not been established.
- ABS, TCS, ESP, countersteering and the existing stability helpers remain. The launch tests record zero raw and processed steering, distinguishing the observed pull from an input/steering-assist request. No artificial yaw forces or velocity correction were added.

## Progression

Progression formulas are unchanged and derive from the corrected prefab baseline. At level 3: torque +9%, brakes +12%, suspension spring +4.5%, damping +10.5% (4972.5), antiroll +7.5%, downforce +12%, drag +3%. Handling no longer depends on buying upgrades to compensate for the unstable baseline. Stock G01 events intentionally enforce level 0.

## Verification

Validation is performed in Unity 6000.3.19f1, using isolated Formula save paths. Older diagnosis files in this folder describe intermediate configurations; they are not acceptance evidence for the final prefab.

- `CorneringBalance.json`: **passed 86 cases**, no errors. Fresh vehicles at 60/100/140/200/260 km/h, taps, sustained turns, reversals and lifting mid-turn; level 0 and level 3; short launches with and without assists. Production peak slip angle 8.75 degrees, all four wheels grounded throughout. Stock sustained-turn heading increased from 33.64 to 37.48 degrees at 100 km/h, 22.91 to 30.62 at 140, and 16.68 to 23.38 at 200. Both comparison groups use the final chassis; this isolates the steering change against the earlier restrictive curve.
- `KeyboardLap.json`: **passed again after the traction correction**, valid stock G01 career lap in 93.29 s, peak speed 172.51 km/h, peak slip 5.38 degrees. Virtual keyboard through actual W/A/S/D bindings, no AI/pose/velocity injection during the lap; normal checkpoint validation, award and post-finish AI takeover. AI rack speed remained 10 and player traction protection reset after takeover. The trace includes 147 samples with full throttle and more than 0.35 degrees of wheel steering, and 144 samples with torque protection below 0.95.
- `KeyboardLap-LegacyDriver.json` retains a failed diagnostic run. Its old render-frame duty-cycle driver assumed immediate wheel response and was incompatible with the asymmetric rack travel. The test now makes held/released binary decisions once per observed physics step using measured rack angle. Route, 65–170 km/h speed plan and acceptance conditions were not weakened; production handling was unchanged between these two runs. Both device tests now record route/input/rack/slip/grounding traces every approximately 0.2 s.
- `GamepadLap.json`: **passed again after the traction correction**, valid stock G01 lap in 94.14 s, peak speed 172.19 km/h, peak slip 4.73 degrees. Virtual gamepad through the actual analog stick/trigger bindings: 4090 fractional-steering samples and 2502 fractional-trigger samples. Post-finish AI rack speed remained 10 and player traction protection reset after takeover. The run recorded continuous application focus and no errors; 72 trace samples combined full throttle and wheel steering, and protection was active in 167 trace samples.
- `GamepadLap-FocusInterruption.json` retains an earlier failed run in which all raw controls dropped to zero around 59 s. RCCP disables its input actions on focus loss, which is the suspected explanation; that first run did not record focus telemetry and cannot prove it. The harness now explicitly reports focus loss as `interrupted`. The successful repeat changed diagnostics only, not production handling or acceptance.
- `LaunchCalibrationComparison.json`: **passed** on the actual saved prefabs with normal progression. Twelve-second full-throttle launches, zero requested/processed steer, all four wheels grounded. On level ground, final lateral displacement was 0.079 m at L0 and 0.094 m at L3, versus 11.507 m for the recreated old chassis configuration. On a 1.6-degree bank, L0/L3 displacement was -0.856/+1.820 m with heading change 0.585/1.900 degrees, versus 89.535 m and 34.853 degrees for the control. Selected profiles reached over 206 km/h. The control restores old damping/force-point/COM at runtime; both groups retain the prior centered-wheel correction. Single-run magnitudes vary, so the acceptance bounds and earlier diagnosis runs matter more than the exact centimetres.
- `AIProfiles.json`: **passed** the eight-second, three-opponent navigation and steering-ownership check; opponents moved 145–152 m and kept their AI steering calibration. This is an ownership/navigation regression check, not a full race or collision-free AI handling assessment.
- `GridLaunchValidation.json`: **passed** the natural stock G01 grid start with W only. Over five seconds and about 72 m, peak lateral displacement was 0.00083 m and heading deviation 0.011 degrees; speed reached 105.63 km/h. Raw steering, processed steering and front-wheel angle stayed zero. No pose or velocity writes, AI, A or D were used.

The six reports above have zero recorded errors; the cornering/launch/AI reports predate the subsequent traction component, whose acceptance is recorded separately in `PowerCornerReview.md`. Editor compilation and `git diff --check` passed. Static inspection confirms the same chassis/steering settings and exactly one enabled traction component in all nine Generic prefabs. Preexisting workspace changes were preserved; four materials also retain Unity-generated motion-vector serialization changes unrelated to handling.

Physical hardware feel, steering-wheel peripherals and standalone PC/Switch builds are outside these automated Editor checks. The subsequent traction suite covers measured render rates of approximately 30, 60 and 118 FPS in this Editor with 50 Hz physics.
